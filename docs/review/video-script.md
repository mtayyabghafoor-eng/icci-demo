# Video: script, shot list and how to make it

**Length:** about 4½ minutes. **Language:** your voice in English, Arabic
subtitles on screen. **Audience:** the UAE funding committee and the
minister.

**The story in one line:** a new administration of three people is giving
the Centre a fresh start, and this system makes sure everything is accounted
for, every decision is recorded, and every euro can be traced.

**One rule on tone:** never mention the past audit, the closure or the
misuse of funds. Everyone watching knows. Speak only about the fresh start
and how trust is protected from now on.

Under each line you will find the Arabic subtitle for it. Ask a native
Arabic speaker to read the Arabic once before the video is sent.

---

## Part A: the scenes

Each scene says what is on screen, what you say, and the Arabic subtitle.
Speak slowly, about two words a second. Pause for one breath between
sentences; it makes editing and subtitles much easier.

### Scene 1: Title card (5 seconds, no voice)
**Screen:** dark green title card: "ICCI Control Dashboard" and, under it,
"A fresh start, fully accounted for".

> لوحة التحكم الإدارية للمركز الثقافي الإسلامي في أيرلندا
> بداية جديدة، وكل شيء موثّق

### Scene 2: Who you are (15 seconds)
**Screen:** you on camera (laptop webcam is fine), or the sign-in page if you
prefer not to appear. Being on camera builds trust; recommended.

> "Assalamu alaikum. My name is [your name]. I am a doctor and a member of
> the community at Clonskeagh, and I built this system to support the
> Centre's new administration."

> السلام عليكم. اسمي [اسمك]. أنا طبيب وأحد أبناء الجالية في كلونسكي، وقد بنيت هذا النظام لدعم الإدارة الجديدة للمركز.

> "Everything you will see inside the system uses made-up names and
> records."

> جميع الأسماء والسجلات التي ستشاهدونها داخل النظام وهمية لأغراض العرض.

### Scene 3: The situation today (40 seconds)
**Screen:** your visit photos, 4 seconds each, slow zoom: the cluttered
storeroom shelves, the boxes of mugs and CDs, the paper "Mimbar Store"
sheets, the stacked tables, the worn chairs, the minibus from the back
(number plate blurred or cropped). No faces.

> "Today, just three people run this entire Centre."

> اليوم، ثلاثة أشخاص فقط يديرون هذا المركز بأكمله.

> "They have inherited storerooms full of items that were never recorded:
> furniture, equipment, kitchen supplies, books, even a minibus."

> وقد ورثوا مخازن مليئة بأغراض لم تُسجَّل قط: أثاث ومعدات ولوازم مطبخ وكتب، وحتى حافلة صغيرة.

> "Some lists are on paper. Most things are on no list at all."

> بعض القوائم على الورق، ومعظم الأشياء غير مسجّلة في أي قائمة.

> "When something breaks, someone mentions it in passing. The admin goes on
> site, calls a tradesman, and often pays from his own pocket."

> وعندما يتعطّل شيء، يذكره أحدهم عابراً، فيذهب المسؤول بنفسه، ويتصل بالفنّي، وكثيراً ما يدفع من جيبه الخاص.

> "Without records, it is hard to say what belongs to the Centre, and hard to
> show anyone how money is spent."

> ومن دون سجلات، يصعب تحديد ما يملكه المركز، ويصعب أن نُثبت لأي أحد كيف تُصرف الأموال.

### Scene 4: The answer (10 seconds)
**Screen:** the home page on the laptop.

> "This system gives the new administration a fresh start: every item
> accounted for, every decision recorded, every euro traceable."

> هذا النظام يمنح الإدارة الجديدة بداية جديدة: كل غرض موثّق، وكل قرار مسجّل، وكل يورو يمكن تتبّعه.

### Scene 5: Recording what is there (40 seconds)
**Screen:** laptop: Items, then "Register an item": fill in the label number,
the kind and a name, choose a room, save. Then the phone: scan that label;
the item's page opens.

> "The first big job is to record what is there. The admin puts a small QR
> label on each item and registers it in under a minute: what it is, which
> room it is in, and its condition."

> المهمة الكبرى الأولى هي تسجيل ما هو موجود. يضع المسؤول ملصقاً صغيراً برمز QR على كل غرض، ويسجّله في أقل من دقيقة: ما هو، وفي أي غرفة، وما حالته.

> "From then on, anyone on the team scans the label with a phone and sees the
> item's full record and history."

> ومن بعدها، يمسح أي عضو في الفريق الملصق بهاتفه، فيرى السجل الكامل للغرض وتاريخه.

> "Room by room, storerooms of unknown items become a proper register."

> وغرفةً بعد غرفة، تتحوّل المخازن المجهولة إلى سجلّ منظّم.

### Scene 6: Decisions need two people (40 seconds)
**Screen:** laptop: open an asset, choose "Dispose", give a reason, submit.
Sign out, sign in as the second admin, open asset approvals, approve.

> "Once an item is on the register, it is the Centre's, on the record. No one
> can later say it was theirs."

> وبمجرد تسجيل الغرض، يصبح ملكاً للمركز بشكل موثّق، ولا يستطيع أحد لاحقاً أن يدّعي أنه له.

> "If it is to be given in charity, disposed of, or sold, the reason is
> recorded, and a second admin must approve it. Nobody can approve their own
> request."

> وإذا تقرّر التبرّع به صدقةً، أو التخلّص منه، أو بيعه، يُسجَّل السبب، ويجب أن يوافق عليه مسؤول ثانٍ. لا يستطيع أحد أن يوافق على طلبه بنفسه.

> "This rule is built into the database itself, so it cannot be switched
> off. And nothing is ever deleted; every change is kept, with who did it and
> when."

> وهذه القاعدة مبنية في قاعدة البيانات نفسها، فلا يمكن تعطيلها. ولا يُحذف أي شيء أبداً؛ كل تغيير محفوظ، مع اسم من قام به ووقته.

### Scene 7: The minibus (20 seconds)
**Screen:** Vehicles list, then one vehicle's page (made-up data).

> "Take the Centre's minibus. When it is sold, the system will hold the
> decision, both approvals, the price and the receipt, so the sale is lawful
> and open to any audit."

> خذوا مثلاً حافلة المركز الصغيرة. عند بيعها، سيحفظ النظام القرار والموافقتين والسعر والإيصال، ليكون البيع نظامياً ومفتوحاً لأي تدقيق.

### Scene 8: Supplies (20 seconds)
**Screen:** phone: scan the tissues label, enter a count, save. Laptop: the
low-stock list.

> "For supplies like tissues, soap and tea, the admin counts the shelf on the
> phone. The system works out what was used, and warns before anything runs
> out."

> أما اللوازم مثل المناديل والصابون والشاي، فيعدّ المسؤول ما على الرف من هاتفه. ويحسب النظام ما استُهلك، وينبّه قبل نفاد أي شيء.

### Scene 9: People and policies (25 seconds)
**Screen:** People, then Garda vetting, then the policy grid.

> "The team will grow from three people to around twenty. The system keeps
> track of who has valid Garda vetting, whose training is due, and who has
> signed each policy. Red means it needs attention now."

> وسيكبر الفريق من ثلاثة أشخاص إلى نحو عشرين. ويتابع النظام من لديه تدقيق أمني ساري المفعول، ومن حان موعد تدريبه، ومن وقّع على كل سياسة. اللون الأحمر يعني أن الأمر يحتاج إلى متابعة الآن.

### Scene 10: What comes next (25 seconds)
**Screen:** the home page; or your photo of the stained carpet, then the home
page.

> "The next stages add repairs reported in the system instead of by word of
> mouth, and every expense recorded with its receipt and approved by a second
> person."

> وتضيف المراحل القادمة الإبلاغ عن الأعطال داخل النظام بدلاً من الكلام الشفهي، وتسجيل كل مصروف مع إيصاله وموافقة شخص ثانٍ عليه.

> "When Revenue or an auditor asks a question, the answer is already there."

> وعندما تسأل مصلحة الضرائب أو أي جهة تدقيق، يكون الجواب جاهزاً.

### Scene 11: For the funders (20 seconds)
**Screen:** the home page.

> "For you, our funders, it means confidence. Each month you will receive a
> one-page report showing the Centre's assets, stock, repairs, compliance and
> spending."

> وبالنسبة لكم، أيها المموّلون الكرام، يعني ذلك الثقة. ففي كل شهر ستصلكم صفحة واحدة تعرض ممتلكات المركز ومخزونه وإصلاحاته والتزامه ومصروفاته.

### Scene 12: Close (20 seconds)
**Screen:** you on camera, or the sign-in page with the ICCI logo. End on the
title card.

> "The system is secure, hosted in Europe, and will be owned by the Centre.
> The first stages are already built and working. With your support, we can
> complete it and put it into daily use."

> النظام آمن، ومستضاف في أوروبا، وسيكون ملكاً للمركز. والمراحل الأولى مبنية وتعمل الآن. وبدعمكم نستطيع إكماله ووضعه في الاستخدام اليومي.

> "Thank you. JazakAllahu khayran."

> شكراً لكم، وجزاكم الله خيراً.

## Part B: before you record (30 minutes)

1. **Photos:** pick 6 to 8 from the visit (storeroom shelves, mug and CD
   boxes, the paper store sheets, stacked tables, worn chairs, the minibus
   from the back). Ask the admin's permission to use them. Crop out faces
   and the bus number plate (Photos app, Edit, Crop).
2. **Labels:** print one label sheet from the demo site. Stick labels on a
   kettle (or any appliance), a chair, and a box of tissues.
3. **Sign in** on the laptop as the Centre Admin and on the phone as the same
   account, before you press record. Have the second admin account ready in
   another browser (for example Edge) so you only switch windows, never type
   a password on camera.
4. **Phone:** Do Not Disturb on, brightness up.
5. **Laptop:** close other tabs; browser zoom 110%.
6. **Quiet room**, phone earphones with a microphone if you have them (much
   clearer voice than the laptop's own microphone).
7. **Never on screen:** passwords, sign-in codes, gate codes, real names,
   number plates.

## Part C: recording (about 1 hour)

Record **pictures and voice separately**. It is far easier: you can click
calmly without talking, and read the script calmly without clicking.

**1. The screen clips (no talking).** In Clipchamp: Record & create, Screen,
pick the browser window, Share, red button. One clip per scene (scenes 4 to
11). Move the mouse slowly; wait two seconds after each click.

**2. The phone clips.** Android: swipe down twice, Screen recorder. iPhone:
Control Centre, the round record button. One clip per phone action (scene 5
scan, scene 8 count). Send them to the laptop (WhatsApp to yourself or a USB
cable).

**3. You on camera (scenes 2 and 12).** Clipchamp: Record & create, Camera.
Look at the camera dot, not the screen. Three or four tries is normal.

**4. The voice.** Clipchamp: Record & create, Audio. Read one scene at a
time from this script. If you stumble, pause, and say the sentence again;
you will cut the bad one later.

## Part D: putting it together (1 to 2 hours)

1. **New video** in Clipchamp, 16:9.
2. **Title card:** Text (left), pick a plain style, drag it to the start, type
   the title. Background: Content library, Backgrounds, a solid dark green.
3. **Lay the voice clips** on the timeline in scene order.
4. **Lay the picture clips** above the voice, each lined up with its scene.
   If a picture clip is longer than the voice, cut it: click it, move the
   playhead, press S to split, select the extra piece, Delete. If it is
   shorter, slow it down (Speed, 0.75x) or hold the last frame.
5. **Photos (scene 3):** drag each photo in, set it to about 4 seconds, and
   add Filters or Effects, "Zoom in" (gentle movement looks professional).
6. **Phone clips:** they are tall. Place them in the middle with the dark
   green background behind; that looks deliberate.
7. **Arabic subtitles:** see Part E.
8. **English captions** (optional but helpful): click the voice clip,
   Captions on the right, Turn on auto captions, then read them and fix any
   wrong words.
9. **Music:** none, or very quiet. Your voice matters more.

## Part E: the Arabic subtitles

The Arabic for every sentence is ready as **picture files**, one per
sentence, in `docs/review/video-subtitles/` (numbered in order, for example
`03-2.png` is scene 3, second sentence). Pictures avoid any problem with
Arabic letters not joining properly in the video editor.

1. Drag all the subtitle pictures into Clipchamp (Import media).
2. For each sentence: drag its picture onto the top track, above the video.
3. Line it up with the moment you start saying that sentence, and stretch it
   to the moment you finish.
4. Position: bottom of the screen, full width (it is already sized for a
   1920 x 1080 video; choose "Fit" if asked).
5. Play the whole video once and check every subtitle appears with its
   sentence.

If you change the wording when you speak, tell Claude Code what you said and
the matching subtitle will be redone.

## Part F: finish and check (15 minutes)

1. **Export**, 1080p.
2. Watch it once on your phone, start to finish, with the sound off. If you
   can follow it from the Arabic alone, it works for the minister.
3. Show it to one person who has not seen the system. Ask them: "What does
   it do, and why does the Centre need it?" If they can answer, send it.
4. Send it as a file, or upload to YouTube as **Unlisted** (only people with
   the link can see it) and send the link.
